Five Knights

Rules: Five knights — A, B, C, D, E — are electing a leader from among themselves. Each knight has a personal ranking of all five knights, from favorite to least favorite. All five vote simultaneously for one knight each.
  • A knight is elected if they get strictly more votes than every other knight. Even 2 votes can be enough if nobody else reaches 2.
  • If there's a tie for the most votes, nobody is elected.
Payoffs: +2 if your 1st choice is elected, +1 for your 2nd choice, 0 if nobody is elected, −1 / −2 / −3 for your 3rd / 4th / 5th choice.

Each knight's card shows the payoff they'd get for voting each way, given everyone else's current vote. A knight is happy (green ring) if their current vote is already their best response, and unhappy (red ring) if switching would improve their payoff. When every knight is happy at once, the votes form a Nash equilibrium.

Note: This applet does not model the moves of the game, but rather gives a visual representation of whether a given outcome is a Nash equilibrium. Try to find a Nash equilibrium by choosing best responses for each knight.
