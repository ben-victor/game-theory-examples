Random NIM(n,p) vs Computer

Rules: Same as NIM(n) — you and the computer alternate removing one or two stones from a single row, only ever from the right-hand end, and whoever cannot move because no stones are left loses. You move first.

The twist: after every move (yours or the computer's), before the other player's turn, nature steps in — with probability p it randomly removes one more stone from the row, and with probability 1−p it removes nothing. If that random removal empties the row, the player who was about to move loses, just as if they'd run out of moves normally.

The computer's strategy isn't the simple mod-3 rule from plain NIM — with chance in play, the right move depends on both how many stones remain and the value of p, so the computer uses a set of precomputed thresholds in p for each stone count to play optimally against whatever probability you set below.
