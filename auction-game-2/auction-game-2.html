Two-Item Auction

Rules: Two paintings, whose worth is known in advance, are auctioned in a known order to two bidders, Ann and Beth. Each starts at $1000, rising $1000 per round as they alternate "Yes" (raise) / "Pass" (drop out). After one passes, the other gets the painting — but then the one who passed gets first offer on the next painting. If Ann passes on the very first $1000 bid, Beth also gets to weigh in at $1000; if she passes too, nobody gets that painting, and the next one starts with whichever of them didn't pass last.

You can bid more than you have. It only matters at the very end: if your final cash balance is negative once both paintings are sold, you're bankrupt and your payoff is counted as −100,000, no matter how much painting value you hold. Otherwise your payoff is your final cash plus the value of paintings you won, minus what you started with. Neither bidder cares how the other does — only about maximizing their own payoff.
