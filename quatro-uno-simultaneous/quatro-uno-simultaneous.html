Simultaneous Quatro-Uno

Rules: You and the computer each have the cards 1, 2, 3, 4. Secretly and simultaneously, you both arrange your four cards into a pile, front to back — this is a single blind decision, not something you can react to.
  • Each round, both piles reveal their front card.
  • The higher card wins and stays at the front to guard again next round; the lower one is discarded.
  • Equal cards are both discarded.
  • Special rule: a 1 facing a 4 also discards both, even though 4 is higher.
Whoever's pile empties first loses. The computer isn't reacting to you — it commits to one of four fixed orderings drawn from its game-theoretically optimal mixed strategy (20% / 40% / 20% / 20%), the same equilibrium this game's textbook chapter works out by hand.
